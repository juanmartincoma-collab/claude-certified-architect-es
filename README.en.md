# Claude Certified Architect — Spanish-language prep

Practice exams and reasoning guides for Anthropic's two architect certifications: **Foundations (CCAR-F)** and **Professional (CCAR-P)**. Free, no signup, 163 original questions and an interactive simulator that grades and explains every answer.

Written by someone who sat the Professional exam and passed it.

### ▶︎ [Open the simulator](https://juanmartincoma-collab.github.io/claude-certified-architect-es/simulador/)

---

## Why this exists

The exams are English-only. If English is not your first language, the difficulty isn't the architecture — it's reading dense stems against the clock. I sat the Professional in August 2026 and walked out with a list of everything I wish I'd had. This is that list.

The community material that already exists covers **Foundations**, in English or machine-translated. Nothing covered the **Professional**, in any language, in Spanish. Both are here now.

**The material is in Spanish.** Questions and answer options are in English, matching the real exam; every explanation, guide and the glossary are in Spanish. If you read English comfortably, you may still find the reasoning guides useful — they're organised around the distinction between two plausible options, which is where the exam puts you.

## How the questions were built

All questions are **original**, written against the objectives Anthropic publishes in the official exam guide, respecting blueprint weights and format proportions. None reproduces, paraphrases or derives from a real exam item.

Two design decisions worth knowing, because they change how much practice here is worth:

**No length bias.** In many practice sets the correct answer is always the longest one, and you learn to exploit that instead of reasoning. Here the correct option is the longest in 29% of items, against the 25% pure chance would give. Within an item, longest and shortest option differ by seven characters on average, so there is nothing to measure.

**No tell-tale clauses.** No option carries its own justification. Every option is a flat assertion of the same length and grammatical shape.
